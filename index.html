Nutrition & Healthy Eating in Australia

Australia is considered one of the healthiest countries in the world (Huang, Q., 2025). But how well do Australians align with Nutrition and Healthy Eating recommendations? This dashboard explores dietary habits and nutritional guidelines among Australians, ultimately revealing how they compare with the recommended dietary standards.

Australian Dietary Guidelines

The Australian Dietary Guidelines provide consumption recommendations and nutrition requirements across 5 different food groups:

In 2011-12, the best performing food group was fruit, with 30.7% of Australians meeting the recommended intake. Being the highest result, this indicates that less than one third of Australians meet any of the dietary standards. This inability to meet nutritional guidelines continued in 2023, with the best performing food group of Grains & Cereals being met by just 27.8% of Australians.

Three out of five of the food groups including Fruit, Grains & Cereals actually declined over the period, indicating that Australians diets and nutrition intake are getting worse. The Fruit consumption saw the largest decline of 7.6%.

Lean meat and alternatives showed the largest improvement, increasing from 14.1% to 20.6%. However, even with this increase, only approximately one in five Australians met the recommended intake.

Fruit & Vegetable Consumption

One way we can look at healthy eating across Australia is by observing the population meeting the fruit and vegetable recommendations. Looking at graph Percentage of People Not Meeting Fruit and Vegetable Consumption Recommendations by State, we can observe that all the states of Australia have more than half their state population not meeting the consumption recommendations. Northern Territory have the worst statistics with 57.2% not meeting consumption whereas New South Wales has the best percentage with 52.2%.

While the map shows how all Australians are performing at a state level, when observing the adult population only in the Australian's over 18 not meeting Fruit & Vegetable Recommendations graph, the percentages are much higher. The Fruit & Vegetables line ranges between 94.6% and 95.8% throughout the 15 year period, meaning only 4.2-5.4% of Australian adults were meeting both recommendations over this time. Given the worst state level statistic was 57.2%, significantly lower than that of the lowest adult only statistic of 94.6%, this suggests that children and teenagers are driving much of the fruit and vegetable consumption across Australia.

Observing vegetable intake alone, consumption remains significantly low, with over 92.4% of Australian adults failing to meet the recommended intake across the entire period.

Fruit intake is significantly better, with around half of adults meeting the recommendations. In 2007-08 and 2017-18, more than 50% of adults were meeting the fruit consumption guideline. However, in 2022, this figure accelerated to the highest level over the period of 55.8%, which indicates Australian’s are only getting worse at meeting these nutritional standards.

Dietary Habits

Given Australian’s aren’t eating their recommended fruits and vegetables, what are they eating? The food group that recorded the highest percentage of Australian’s meeting the recommendation was Grains & Cereals, with 27.3%. Females record higher percentages than males in serving sizes below 5 serves per day. From there, males have a greater percentage of consumption. The smallest difference between males and females occurs at 4 to <5 serves, while the largest difference occurs between 2 to <3 serves.

As previously established, there was a 6.5% increase in Lean Meats & Alternatives consumption from 2012 to 2023. What foods contribute most to Australians' lean meat and alternatives intake? Red and white meats make up the largest proportion of this food group, with 33.9% and 30.1% respectively, while nuts and seeds (12.8%), fish and seafood (9.8%), and eggs (8.6%) all exceed the lowest contributor, legumes including tofu (4.8%).

A food group that Australian’s are excessively incorporating into their diet is Unsaturated Spreads and Oils. 51.3% of the Australian Population are exceeding the recommended intake for this category, with 42.0% of females exceeding the guideline compared with 60.9% of males who surpass this allowance.

Which states are the healthiest?

iSelect have come up with an index score from 0-10, to gauge Australia’s healthiest eaters. According to these scores, Northern Territory is the unhealthiest state with an index score of 3.43. Western Australia is recorded as the healthiest state with an index score of 6.58, Victoria has the second lowest index score of 4.29.

Another way we can observe which states are eating healthy is observing the google trend searches of each state. Values are calculated on a scale from 0 to 100, where 100 is the location with the most popularity as a fraction of total searches in that location. In this case Victoria has recorded the largest relative search interest with a score of 100. New South Wales and Queensland also showed high interest in health related searches with scores of 96 and 98 respectively. Again Northern Territory remained the lowest score, with a score of 71.
